1 Abstract

CostConformal-Return converts e-commerce return prediction into a value-sensitive intervention policy. The ASOS benchmark event, customer-node and product-node data are validated, joined safely, preprocessed, explored and modelled with a broad machine-learning model zoo. Leading models are tuned and their return probabilities are calibrated before being passed to Conformal Risk Control (CRC).

The final decision layer does not use an arbitrary probability cutoff. CRC selects a policy threshold for a chosen risk target. The risk is value-weighted missed-return exposure: the proportion of return value left untreated by the intervention policy. This connects the statistical result to a practical business decision: how much intervention workload is required to keep missed-return exposure below an acceptable level.

The final project evaluates global and category-aware CRC policies, subgroup intervention burden, future-period drift, calibration stability and paired-bootstrap statistical comparisons between XGBoost and Elastic-Net. It therefore provides both predictive evidence and policy assurance.

2 1. Introduction

E-commerce returns create reverse-logistics costs, reduce availability and affect customer operations. A conventional classifier gives a likelihood of return, but a deployable system needs more: calibrated probabilities, a defensible intervention threshold, awareness that orders differ in value, and evidence that the policy remains safe outside the development sample.

The project answers the following research question:

Can calibrated machine-learning probabilities be transformed into an intervention policy that controls value-weighted missed-return exposure at a chosen risk level?

The project has three phases.

  1. Phase 1: Data conversion, validation, leakage-safe joins and splits, preprocessing, exploratory analysis and baseline CRC.
  2. Phase 2: Feature engineering, SQLite connectivity, a model zoo, hyperparameter tuning and model selection.
  3. Phase 3: Final calibration, global/category-aware CRC, subgroup and future-period audit, and bootstrap statistical comparison.

3 2. Dataset Description

3.1 2.1 Source data and unit of analysis

The benchmark contains event records together with customer and product node tables, for an official training period and a later official future-test period. Each row is an event/order observation with binary return outcome isReturned. Customer and product variables are joined using valid identifiers. Return-reason ratio variables are intentionally excluded because they would reveal information after the outcome and introduce leakage.

5 4. Statistical Analysis

5.1 4.1 Descriptive analysis

The descriptive evidence comprises event counts, class proportions, product-type rates and confidence intervals, price/outcome summaries, customer-history summaries, discount bins, missingness reports and time-period comparison. Together they show that the data are large, heterogeneous and operationally relevant.

  • Return prevalence is near 55% across the main modelling splits.
  • Return rates differ substantially by product type.
  • Customer and product historical rates contain meaningful behavioural signal.
  • Product-join gaps are material enough to require explicit missingness handling.
  • A future period is available, enabling stronger temporal evaluation than a single random holdout.

The following graph-by-graph inferences are also placed directly below their figures in the Word report.

  1. Return class distribution: Returns are close to 55% of events, so probability quality and cost-sensitive missed exposure matter beyond accuracy.
  2. Product price by outcome: Price distributions overlap, so price is useful with other predictors rather than as a standalone rule.

  4. Customer history density: Historical customer behaviour differs by outcome, confirming its predictive value.
  5. Discount ratio versus return rate: Discount changes observed behaviour but is insufficient for a fixed rule; it belongs in the multivariable model.
  6. Return rate by official period: Even a small future-period shift requires temporal policy testing.
  7. ROC-AUC comparison: XGBoost has the strongest ranking, while close alternatives justify retaining Elastic-Net as a finalist.
  8. PR-AUC comparison: Leading models also separate returned orders well when emphasis is placed on the positive class.
  9. Brier-score comparison: Stronger models have better probability quality, but calibration remains necessary before thresholding.
  10. Tuning gain: Tuning produces incremental gains, making model-family choice and calibration equally important.
  11. Calibration curve: Isotonic calibration improves agreement between predicted and observed return rates.
  12. Exposure/workload trade-off: Stricter risk targets require intervention on more orders.
  13. Category-aware CRC risk: Category thresholds reduce excess risk in several categories; remaining misses require monitoring.
  14. ROC curves: Final candidates discriminate well, with XGBoost showing the best overall curve area.
  15. Precision-recall curves: The models can target high-probability returns effectively for selective interventions.
  16. Confusion matrices: Fixed-cutoff counts reveal the catch-versus-workload trade-off that CRC formalises.
  17. Final policy comparison: Workload falls as permitted risk rises, and the preferred finalist depends on the chosen target.
  18. Bootstrap confidence intervals: Intervals excluding zero show statistically detectable policy differences.

CostConformal-Return is an end-to-end, decision-aware return-prediction project. It goes beyond a high-AUC classifier by integrating data validation, leakage prevention, reproducible preprocessing, feature selection, broad model comparison, tuning, calibrated probabilities and a risk-controlled intervention policy.

The principal conclusions are:

  1. XGBoost is the strongest predictive model on the full validation benchmark; Elastic-Net is a strong structured comparator.
  2. Isotonic calibration is the selected probability-calibration method, enabling threshold decisions based on better probability quality.
  3. CRC makes the business trade-off explicit: stricter risk targets need more intervention workload, while looser targets permit more value-weighted missed exposure.
  4. Category-aware thresholds improve policy granularity but must be paired with empirical subgroup monitoring.
  5. Future evaluation changes the decision picture: low PSI does not guarantee all future policy targets will be met, so the recalibration recommendation is justified.

Recommended next steps are scheduled recalibration using newly labelled outcomes, monitoring subgroup burden, documenting true intervention costs and creating an online scoring service with logged policy decisions and outcomes.

auc_roc <- function(y_true, y_prob) {
  ok <- !is.na(y_true) & !is.na(y_prob)
  y_true <- y_true[ok]; y_prob <- y_prob[ok]
  n_pos <- as.double(sum(y_true == 1)); n_neg <- as.double(sum(y_true == 0))
  r <- rank(y_prob, ties.method = "average")
  (sum(r[y_true == 1]) - n_pos * (n_pos + 1) / 2) / (n_pos * n_neg)
}

brier_score <- function(y_true, y_prob) {
  ok <- !is.na(y_true) & !is.na(y_prob)
  mean((y_prob[ok] - y_true[ok])^2)
}

apply_crc_policy <- function(calibrated_probability, tau) {
  as.integer(calibrated_probability >= tau)
}

value_weighted_missed_exposure <- function(y_true, intervention, value_exposure) {
  missed <- y_true == 1 & intervention == 0
  sum(value_exposure[missed], na.rm = TRUE) / sum(value_exposure[y_true == 1], na.rm = TRUE)
}

# Abstract

CostConformal-Return converts e-commerce return prediction into a value-sensitive intervention policy. The ASOS benchmark event, customer-node and product-node data are validated, joined safely, preprocessed, explored and modelled with a broad machine-learning model zoo. Leading models are tuned and their return probabilities are calibrated before being passed to Conformal Risk Control (CRC).

The final decision layer does not use an arbitrary probability cutoff. CRC selects a policy threshold for a chosen risk target. The risk is value-weighted missed-return exposure: the proportion of return value left untreated by the intervention policy. This connects the statistical result to a practical business decision: how much intervention workload is required to keep missed-return exposure below an acceptable level.

The final project evaluates global and category-aware CRC policies, subgroup intervention burden, future-period drift, calibration stability and paired-bootstrap statistical comparisons between XGBoost and Elastic-Net. It therefore provides both predictive evidence and policy assurance.

# 1. Introduction

E-commerce returns create reverse-logistics costs, reduce availability and affect customer operations. A conventional classifier gives a likelihood of return, but a deployable system needs more: calibrated probabilities, a defensible intervention threshold, awareness that orders differ in value, and evidence that the policy remains safe outside the development sample.

The project answers the following research question:

> Can calibrated machine-learning probabilities be transformed into an intervention policy that controls value-weighted missed-return exposure at a chosen risk level?

The project has three phases.

1.  **Phase 1:** Data conversion, validation, leakage-safe joins and splits, preprocessing, exploratory analysis and baseline CRC.
2.  **Phase 2:** Feature engineering, SQLite connectivity, a model zoo, hyperparameter tuning and model selection.
3.  **Phase 3:** Final calibration, global/category-aware CRC, subgroup and future-period audit, and bootstrap statistical comparison.

auc_roc <- function(y_true, y_prob) {
  ok <- !is.na(y_true) & !is.na(y_prob)
  y_true <- y_true[ok]; y_prob <- y_prob[ok]
  n_pos <- as.double(sum(y_true == 1)); n_neg <- as.double(sum(y_true == 0))
  r <- rank(y_prob, ties.method = "average")
  (sum(r[y_true == 1]) - n_pos * (n_pos + 1) / 2) / (n_pos * n_neg)
}

brier_score <- function(y_true, y_prob) {
  ok <- !is.na(y_true) & !is.na(y_prob)
  mean((y_prob[ok] - y_true[ok])^2)
}

apply_crc_policy <- function(calibrated_probability, tau) {
  as.integer(calibrated_probability >= tau)
}

}
